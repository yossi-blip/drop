What happens in the first 72 hours after discharge

The first three days home are a high-risk transition. Medications change, wounds need watching, and follow-up care has to be in place before small problems become a trip back to the hospital.

Why this window carries the highest readmission risk

Common causes of readmission include medication errors, infections and falls. Knowing the symptoms to watch for, and who to call, makes the difference.

First 72 hours: what to watch for

WhenFocus
Discharge dayConfirm every medication and the discharge instructions; make sure follow-up appointments are booked.
Days 1–2Monitor wounds, symptoms and medication timing; watch for warning signs.
Day 3 and afterKeep follow-up appointments and adjust the level of support as recovery progresses.

What hospital case managers do (and don't) handle

Case managers arrange post-discharge medical services. Ongoing daily help at home usually has to be arranged separately, and that's where we come in.

Types of post-discharge home care support

The right type and length of care depends on the diagnosis and recovery needs.

Personal care aides for daily living

Non-medical help with bathing, mobility, meals and similar tasks. About our aides.

Skilled nursing for wound care and medications

Licensed nursing such as wound checks, infection monitoring and medication teaching. About skilled nursing.

Physical, occupational and speech therapy

Therapy rebuilds strength and mobility, everyday skills, and communication or swallowing. About therapy.

Signs your family member needs more than family support

Professional help is worth considering when your loved one needs hands-on care that family can't safely provide, or when no one is available during the day.

Paying for post-discharge home care in Brooklyn

Options include Medicare, Medicaid, private insurance and out-of-pocket payment.

Medicaid-funded PCA and MLTC options

Medicaid may cover aide services through an assessment and a managed long-term care (MLTC) plan.

Medicare's short-term skilled care coverage

Medicare covers short-term skilled nursing and therapy, but generally not long-term daily help.
