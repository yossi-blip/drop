<h2 id="a-72">What happens in the first 72 hours after discharge</h2>
<p>The first three days home are a high-risk transition. Medications change, wounds need watching, and follow-up care has to be in place before small problems become a trip back to the hospital.</p>
<h3>Why this window carries the highest readmission risk</h3>
<p>Common causes of readmission include medication errors, infections and falls. Knowing the symptoms to watch for, and who to call, makes the difference.</p>

<h2 id="a-watch">First 72 hours: what to watch for</h2>
<div class="tbl-wrap"><table class="tbl" style="min-width:520px">
<thead><tr><th scope="col">When</th><th scope="col">Focus</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">Discharge day</th><td>Confirm every medication and the discharge instructions; make sure follow-up appointments are booked.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Days 1–2</th><td>Monitor wounds, symptoms and medication timing; watch for warning signs.</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">Day 3 and after</th><td>Keep follow-up appointments and adjust the level of support as recovery progresses.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<h3>What hospital case managers do (and don't) handle</h3>
<p>Case managers arrange post-discharge medical services. Ongoing daily help at home usually has to be arranged separately, and that's where we come in.</p>

<h2 id="a-types">Types of post-discharge home care support</h2>
<p>The right type and length of care depends on the diagnosis and recovery needs.</p>
<h3>Personal care aides for daily living</h3>
<p>Non-medical help with bathing, mobility, meals and similar tasks. <a href="#home-health-aides">About our aides</a>.</p>
<h3>Skilled nursing for wound care and medications</h3>
<p>Licensed nursing such as wound checks, infection monitoring and medication teaching. <a href="#skilled-nursing">About skilled nursing</a>.</p>
<h3>Physical, occupational and speech therapy</h3>
<p>Therapy rebuilds strength and mobility, everyday skills, and communication or swallowing. <a href="#therapy">About therapy</a>.</p>

<h2 id="a-signs">Signs your family member needs more than family support</h2>
<p>Professional help is worth considering when your loved one needs hands-on care that family can't safely provide, or when no one is available during the day.</p>

<h2 id="a-pay">Paying for post-discharge home care in Brooklyn</h2>
<p>Options include Medicare, Medicaid, private insurance and out-of-pocket payment.</p>
<h3>Medicaid-funded PCA and MLTC options</h3>
<p>Medicaid may cover aide services through an assessment and a managed long-term care (MLTC) plan.</p>
<h3>Medicare's short-term skilled care coverage</h3>
<p>Medicare covers short-term skilled nursing and therapy, but generally not long-term daily help.</p>

<h2 id="a-ask">Questions to ask before choosing a Brooklyn home care agency</h2>
<ul class="ticks">
<li>How quickly can care start?</li>
<li>Which services does my coverage include?</li>
<li>How are caregivers trained and supervised?</li>
<li>How will you keep our family and doctors updated?</li>
<li>Are you licensed and accredited?</li>
</ul>
